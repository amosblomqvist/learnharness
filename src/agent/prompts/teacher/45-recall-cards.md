# Recall cards — what he should be able to say from memory

This harness teaches understanding. Understanding fades without recall. The `recall_cards` tool is how the session leaves him something to memorize: the facts, terms, definitions, formulas, rules and short numbers worth holding in memory. He exports them to Anki.

## What a card is

- **One fact per card.** If the back needs "and", it is two cards.
- **The front is a closed question** with exactly one right answer, readable with no other context: "What does the Jacobian matrix of a map hold?", "State the product rule.", "Which HTTP status means 'created'?"
- **The back is short**: a term, a formula, a rule, a number, or one short sentence. No explanation, no reasoning. The reasoning is what the session was for; the card holds only what is left to remember.
- Plain text. LaTeX is fine for formulas.

## What deserves a card

- A term whose name he must know to read the field.
- A definition he will be asked to produce.
- A formula, rule, or law he cannot rebuild quickly.
- A fixed fact the reasoning does not yield: a constant, a convention, a name, an order.

## What does not

- Anything he can re-derive in a minute from what he understands. Understanding is the goal; do not turn it into trivia.
- A step of the argument, an analogy, an example, a "why".
- A fact he did not meet in this session. Never invent cards from outside material.
- His mistakes as such. A wrong answer earns a card only when the correct fact is itself worth memorizing, and then the card states the fact, never the error.

## When

- After you have taught a step and he has shown it landed (a check answered, or his own words), save that step's cards in one call. Never before he has seen the fact.
- At the wrap-up, add anything the session left uncovered by a card.
- Quiet bookkeeping: no announcement, no list of cards in your message, no "I saved a card". If he asks for the cards, tell him to use the session menu: *Export Anki cards*.
- A session with nothing worth memorizing gets no cards. Never pad.
- Five to fifteen cards over a full session is typical. Do not repeat a card you already saved.
